# Activity 10: Run and Debrief a Strategic Sustainability Partnership

**TSC mapping:** K1 · K2 · K3 · K4 · K5 · K6 · A1 · A2 · A3 · A4 · A5  
**Objective:** Integrate preparation, tactics, closure and recording in a complete team negotiation simulation.  
**Expected output:** A signed provisional term sheet or a documented no-deal decision supported by the BATNA.  
**Primary source:** https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/top-10-negotiation-skills/

## Goal

Negotiate a partnership between an urban property operator and a clean-energy solutions company to deploy solar and energy-management services across commercial sites.

## Real-world scenario

Real-world use case: strategic sustainability partnerships combine capital, performance, access, data, branding, risk and long-term governance. The organisations and figures are fictional training inputs.

CivicSquare Properties wants to reduce energy cost and emissions across five sites without major upfront capital. SunGrid Solutions proposes a ten-year performance contract. CivicSquare worries about access, savings verification and exit rights; SunGrid needs scale, roof access and reliable payment. Teams receive different confidential priorities but share a common data pack.

## Evidence boundary

The business setting is a realistic composite. Numerical and organisational inputs created for practice are labelled as training assumptions and must not be presented as real company facts.

### Training assumptions / guardrails

- Estimated project investment is SGD 3.2 million funded primarily by SunGrid.
- Modelled annual savings are SGD 620,000 with a plus/minus 20% uncertainty band.
- CivicSquare can offer five sites but only three have completed structural surveys.
- Both parties require data security, safe access and measurable performance governance.

## Questions

1. What outcomes, BATNAs, roles, evidence and cultural/process assumptions should each team prepare?
2. Which opening package creates a credible anchor across value, risk and implementation?
3. How should teams respond to pressure, impasse and late requests while preserving reciprocity?
4. Does the final record make commitments, owners, evidence and review conditions executable?

## Detailed activity procedure

### Step 1: Prepare separately

Each team completes objectives, interests, BATNA, authority, questions, package and concession plan from its confidential brief.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 2: Assign roles

Name lead, specialist, approver, observer and recorder; agree team signals and escalation rules.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 3: Open and diagnose

Confirm process, exchange priorities, ask diagnostic questions and summarise interests before bargaining.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 4: Create and claim value

Use packages, objective criteria, conditional trades and a supportable anchor; record every movement.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 5: Close or walk away

Confirm the complete package, approvals, owners, dates, measurement and unresolved items—or document why BATNA is preferred.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 6: Debrief with evidence

Compare outcome with target and BATNA; review tactics, relationship, record quality, implementation risk and next improvements.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

## Deliverable

A signed provisional term sheet or a documented no-deal decision supported by the BATNA.

## Acceptance criteria

The submission contains a complete negotiation log and executable term sheet or no-deal rationale, with explicit mapping to A1–A5 and all six knowledge areas.

## Reflection and debrief questions

1. Which assumption changed your negotiation plan most, and why?
2. What evidence would you request before relying on the result?
3. Which stakeholder owns the next decision or approval?
4. What would you repeat or change in the next negotiation?
